# Artifact Design

Make the artifact's visual hierarchy follow the reader's decisions and actions. Choose a small family of useful patterns, test the demanding examples, and apply corrections to the pattern as well as the individual page.

## Scope and references

Preserve the requested medium, established design system, content, and edit scope. Use the relevant document, PDF, presentation, spreadsheet, or web tools for production. For physical cutting, imposition, or handwriting constraints, use available print-layout guidance. This skill supplies cross-format design decisions; it does not require producing additional formats or publishing anything.

Read supporting material selectively:

- [Personal defaults](references/personal-defaults.md): when choosing an unspecified presentation treatment for James. Explicit instructions and an established design system take precedence.
- [Design review](references/design-review.md): when composing a substantial artifact or checking a redesign; use the checks relevant to its medium and purpose.
- [Homebrew dossier case study](references/homebrew-dossier-case-study.md): only when an example, rationale, or dossier-like problem would help. Its equipment, prices, page geometry, and compositions are historical examples.
- [Evaluation cases](references/evaluation-cases.md): when validating or maintaining this skill's scope and behavior.

The general workflow below stands alone. Do not load the case study for every artifact or reproduce its visual theme by default.

## Establish the reader's task

Infer who will use the artifact, what they must understand or do, and where it will be read or handled. Establish consequential format constraints: existing template, dimensions, orientation, viewing distance, page budget, writing space, or device width. Reuse information already available; ask only about consequential unresolved choices.

Distinguish general principles, personal preferences, and choices specific to this assignment. Keep an explanatory annotation addressed to the author separate from intended artifact copy. Write the finished content for its actual reader; keep revision narration and internal bookkeeping outside it unless they help that reader act.

## Model the content before styling it

Choose the meaningful unit: a decision, activity, participant, assembled set, product, or other unit the reader recognizes. A source record, photograph, component, bundle, and reader-facing entry need not be the same unit. Preserve meaningful associations and count shared constituents once where totals depend on them.

Separate identity, included contents, confirmed facts, uncertainty, requested actions, quantitative values, metadata, and evidence. Use only the roles the task needs. For completeness questions, distinguish confirmed absence from an item not yet photographed, located, or identified; keep condition and fit checks separate from missing components.

Lead with the subject, useful finding, or task. Put actions in the action area and lookup identifiers in a secondary role. Prefer familiar, correct names. Remove labels and repetitions that add no identity or context; retain repetition when it saves a useful lookup or supports a different decision.

## Compose a small pattern family

Choose compositions that suit the material's shape and function. Use tables where alignment expresses a relationship, cards for bounded units of comparison or action, and callouts for notes whose importance merits one. Keep pictures and their corresponding text together. Provide recognition views when the reader must identify an unfamiliar object; distinguish actual objects from reference examples and make unresolved matches explicit.

Give callout types stable purposes and appropriate prominence. Financial values, preparation steps, consequential constraints, and supporting references should be distinguishable when present. State their purpose in words; color can reinforce it. Minor bookkeeping should consume little or no reader attention.

Use spacing to express relationships: related elements closer together, separate groups farther apart. Allow comfortable space around decisions and actions; keep lookup tables and secondary directories compact. Before shrinking text to fix overflow, inspect redundancy, grouping, column widths, image sizing, and pagination. Preserve meaningful reading units without forcing every complex topic onto one page.

Choose actual font families and weights deliberately. Use weight, italics, and selective underlining for meaningful emphasis. Verify the rendered faces when typography matters. Judge type size at the intended reading scale and keep a consistent physical page contract where applicable.

## Present numbers, identifiers, and evidence clearly

For each consequential number, state its meaning and basis: unit, quantity, period, condition, or scenario as applicable. Give comparable values parallel treatments. Make the main value unambiguous; subordinate alternative calculations. Make overview totals support the overview's task, with detailed exceptions close to the affected entry.

Introduce only identifiers that improve lookup or cross-reference. Explain necessary families before relying on them, give each key entry its own row, and keep identifiers subordinate to names and values. Use dedicated narrow ID columns where they support table lookup.

A footnote needs an inline anchor and an appropriate page-foot or anchored-note destination. Keep separate notes on separate lines. A supporting reference block needs a name, useful links, and an explanation of what each source establishes. A directory is a compact lookup structure, not an unanchored set of supposed footnotes. Put extended research in an appendix when a concise implication and cross-reference suffice in the main flow.

## Scale, revise, and verify

Before expanding a substantial artifact, test representative demanding units: a long heading, a dense group, an unusual image shape, a quantitative comparison, and long supporting material where present. Choose cases relevant to this assignment rather than manufacturing all of them.

Use reusable patterns and consistent source facts across outputs. Reconcile new evidence through affected contents, statuses, actions, captions, drafts, quantities, and calculations. Translate feedback into its underlying rule, find the other affected instances, and review those instances after changing the pattern.

Check semantic usefulness and rendering separately. Inspect the actual output at its intended scale; check hierarchy, associations, spacing, breaks, typography, links, and requested controls. A web checkbox does not establish PDF interactivity. Ordinary paper checkboxes do not require form fields. Do not add obvious document-use instructions unless the reader needs them.

Scale verification to the change. Review all unique layouts in a new artifact; after a narrow change, inspect affected units and downstream pagination or totals. Structural checks establish dimensions, arithmetic, links, or font presence; they do not establish reader comprehension or optical quality. Describe the checks actually performed and resolve material failures before delivery.
