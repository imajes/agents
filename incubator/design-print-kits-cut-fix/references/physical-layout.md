# Physical layout

Choose the final face before the paper sheet. Half Letter is 396 x 612 points; two unscaled faces fit on landscape Letter, 792 x 612. Exact ISO A5 is 148 x 210 mm; half-A4 is 148.5 x 210 mm. They are different. The helper supports both: exact A5 pairs occupy A4 with a 1 mm center trim strip and two horizontal bars contained in that waste, while half-A4 pairs use one measured center cut with no printed marks. Neither is scaled.

A Letter host sheet may hold a prompt above and numbered private key below. Separate cards, matched paper pieces in a pouch or a fold are alternatives based on actual use. Keep production instructions outside the artwork; don't add fold labels when the user intends to cut.

Print actual size/100%, one PDF page per already-imposed sheet, with automatic portrait/landscape selection for mixed packs. Do not apply another two-up setting. The helper rejects dimensions requiring scaling.

Cut references must survive the sequence, not merely look right on the uncut sheet.

- For an isolated rectangle in waste, use two perpendicular marks per trim edge. Their inner tips end exactly on the trim line; their ink stays wholly in the waste. Inset the pair along the edge so adjacent cuts leave both references attached. Use butt caps, not round or square projecting caps.
- For two game halves sharing an edge, split that shared edge FIRST while its marks still sit in the outer side waste. Each half needs its own repeated left/right trim pairs plus the outer top or bottom pair. Trim those outer edges by aligning to the inner tips. The already-cut shared edge needs no later cut.
- Full half-Letter or half-A4 cards occupy the entire sheet and have no waste for marks. Keep their dimensions and artwork: omit ink guides and provide one measured center cut from the intact sheet edges. Never print a line straddling the finished cards and claim it vanishes.
- Exact A5 on A4 leaves a 1 mm center waste strip. The helper puts two short horizontal bars wholly inside it, with butt-capped tips at both cut positions. After one gutter-edge cut, keep the strip attached to the other card; its opposite tips guide the second cut. Do not trim top/bottom first. A ruler measurement is also valid.

Before delivery, simulate clipping the paper and its printed marks after EVERY cut. Confirm two distinct surviving references (or an explicit intact-edge measurement), the correct finished sizes, and no positive-area guide ink inside any finished rectangle. Exercise reversed adjacent-edge orders and any separate strip/card handling. For grids, plan the strip cuts and repeat marks per strip; isolated-card helpers alone do not prove a grid safe. Include a short cutting sequence beside the deliverable, not in the artwork.

Respect the printer's non-printable margin and verify references lie in printable waste. Starting stroke widths of 0.75–1.2 pt are useful, but stroke width/caps are part of the ink geometry. A successful rendered sheet does not replace a cut-sequence check.

For lamination, cut paper before arranging cards in pouches. Preserve the pouch maker's required seal; don't invent a universal margin. Leave quiet space for a specified grommet and loop, accounting for paper trim and sealed film. Unknown hardware calls for flexible space, not a falsely precise punch guide.

With heavier stock such as 180 gsm, consider cutting prompt and key separately and pairing them back-to-back instead of folding. A tiny amount of suitable adhesive can hold alignment if needed. Check the laminator's maximum total thickness for both paper layers plus the pouch. A user-confirmed successful pouch seal applies to that actual batch, not every thickness combination. Run one card as a trial before assembling another batch, and keep the required sealed film border.

A large screen render is not proof of print legibility. Start handheld text at 12–14 pt; use 9–11 pt only for short subordinate labels or asides where appropriate. When content overflows, edit, reflow, resize or add a face. Preserve useful roster rows and checkboxes.
